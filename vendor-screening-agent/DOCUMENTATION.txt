PLAIN-ENGLISH SUMMARY (read this first)
---------------------------------------
Before anyone creates a NEW supplier ("vendor") record, this tool checks whether
that company already exists - either in the company's own list of vendors, or in
a free public registry of real companies. It gives a match score out of 100, a
plain recommendation ("Likely duplicate", "Human review", or "Likely new"), and
the evidence behind that score. A person then clicks Reuse / Review / Create -
the tool advises, the human decides.

Why it matters: when the same supplier is entered twice under slightly different
names (e.g. "Alpha Consulting" and "Alpha Consulting Ltd."), the company's spend
with that supplier gets split across two records. That breaks spend analysis,
duplicates payments risk, weakens negotiating leverage, and pollutes master
data. Catching duplicates AT THE POINT OF CREATION - before the bad record
exists - is far cheaper than cleaning them up later. That is exactly what this
agent does.


===============================================================================
 1. THE PROBLEM
===============================================================================
Large organisations accumulate duplicate vendor records over time. Two common
ways the SAME real company ends up as TWO records:

  Scenario A - Same company, SIMILAR name (typos, legal suffixes, word order,
               spacing, abbreviations):
                 "Alpha Consulting"      vs "Alpha Consulting Ltd."
                 "ABC Pharma Services"   vs "Services ABC Pharma"
                 "Blue-Tech R&D"         vs "Bluetech R and D"
                 "IBM"                   vs "International Business Machines"

  Scenario B - Same company, DIFFERENT name (brand vs legal name, or a
               subsidiary vs its parent group):
                 "Cognizant"             vs "Cognizant Technology Solutions"
                 "Parent Company"        vs "Parent India Pvt Ltd"
                 a local subsidiary       vs the global parent it belongs to

The business damage is "split spend": purchases are scattered over duplicate
records, so no one sees the true total spent with a supplier. The agent's job is
to flag both scenarios reliably WITHOUT crying wolf on companies that merely
share a word in their name (e.g. "Infosys" is NOT the same company as "Capital
Infosys").


===============================================================================
 2. HOW IT WORKS  -  THE 5-STEP PIPELINE
===============================================================================
In plain language, for every name the user types the agent runs 5 steps:

  1. CLEAN    - Tidy up the name and country (lower-case, strip legal words like
                "Ltd/Pvt/Inc", normalise "&"->"and", turn "India" into the ISO
                code "IN" so it can be compared to what the registry returns).
  2. FIND     - Pull every plausible candidate: fuzzy-match the name against the
                internal vendor master AND search the free GLEIF public registry.
  3. COMPARE  - Score how alike the typed name and each candidate are, using
                three name-matching algorithms blended together, plus an
                acronym check (so IBM = International Business Machines).
  4. VALIDATE - Corroborate with non-name evidence: same country, same city, an
                exact identifier match (LEI), or a matching corporate parent in
                GLEIF. Name-alone can never be a confident duplicate.
  5. RECOMMEND- Produce a score /100, a decision band, per-signal evidence bars,
                and Reuse / Review / Create buttons. A human makes the call.

TECHNICAL DETAIL OF EACH STEP
-----------------------------
Step 1 CLEAN (code: norm, name_tokens, name_core, _iso2, COUNTRY_ISO2):
  - name_core() lower-cases, splits into tokens, and drops STOP_WORDS
    (ltd, pvt, inc, llc, company, technologies, solutions, services, the, and,
     group, ...) then sorts them - so word order and legal suffixes stop
     mattering.
  - Country is normalised to an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code (India->IN, USA->US,
    Germany->DE, ...). This was a real bug fix: GLEIF returns "IN" while the
    user types "India"; comparing them as raw text used to stick the country
    bar at ~57%. Now "India" == "IN" == 100%.

Step 2 FIND (code: internal_matches, gleif_search_by_name):
  - Internal: iterate the vendor master; keep a row only if its name similarity
    is >= 0.55 OR it is an exact LEI match. (Sharing a country alone is NOT a
    reason to surface a record - that used to make every India vendor appear for
    an "Infosys" search.)
  - External: query GLEIF's own server-side fuzzy name search (optionally
    filtered by ISO country code); also resolve by LEI code or ISIN if supplied.

Step 3 COMPARE (code: composite_name_score):
  Three algorithms run on the cleaned names; the BEST of the three is taken:
    - Levenshtein-family (rapidfuzz token_set_ratio) - great for "same words,
      different order / extra words".
    - Jaro-Winkler - rewards matching prefixes; good for typos and punctuation.
    - TF-IDF cosine over character n-grams (scikit-learn) - forgiving of
      spacing/hyphenation, e.g. "Blue-Tech R&D" vs "Bluetech R and D".
  An ACRONYM check runs first and, on a clean hit, overrides everything with
  1.0: "IBM" expands to the initials of "International Business Machines".
  The containment penalty (see section 7) can then damp a misleading 100%.

Step 4 VALIDATE (code: the "corroboration gate" in internal_matches and
  external_matches): see sections 5 and 7.

Step 5 RECOMMEND (code: decision_label, confidence_level, evidence_*): produces
  match_percent, decision band, HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW confidence, five evidence bars,
  a "what's similar / additional info" breakdown, and the decision buttons.

===============================================================================
 4. THE SIGNALS & SCORING
===============================================================================
FIVE EVIDENCE SIGNALS are shown to the user (deck slide 12), each as a bar:
  1. Name similarity      - the blended 3-algorithm name score.
  2. Website / domain     - only when a website is supplied (blank otherwise).
  3. Country / address     - ISO-normalised country comparison.
  4. Registry / identifier - exact LEI match, or GLEIF status (ACTIVE + ISSUED
                             LEI = strong; lapsed = weaker).
  5. External evidence     - the web-search fallback signal (shown "Not checked"
                             when no web search ran, never faked as 0).

INTERNAL WEIGHTED SCORE (code: WEIGHTS):
    name_similarity .50, country_similarity .20, country_match .10,
    city_similarity .10, website_similarity .10
  Only the signals the user actually provided are used, and the weights are
  re-normalised over those - so for a name + country query, name is effectively
  ~62%, country ~38% of the transparent heuristic.
  Final internal score = 0.75 * (transparent heuristic) + 0.25 * (RandomForest
  probability). An exact LEI match forces the score to 100.

EXTERNAL (GLEIF) SCORE (code: external_matches):
    overall = 100 * (0.75 * name_score + 0.25 * country_score)
  An exact LEI match = 100. A matching corporate parent lifts the score to >=90.

DECISION BANDS (the recommendation the user sees):
    85 - 100 : "Likely duplicate"   (HIGH confidence)   -> Reuse existing
    65 - 84  : "Human review"        (MEDIUM confidence) -> a person confirms
    below 65 : "Likely new"          (LOW confidence)    -> safe to create
  Below 40 ("MIN_REPORT_THRESHOLD") a candidate is not shown at all.

HOW THE FINAL RECOMMENDATION IS CHOSEN:
  Internal and GLEIF candidates are pooled and sorted by score; the TOP one
  drives the headline verdict banner. The score must clear the corroboration
  gate (section 7) before it is allowed into the 85-100 "Likely duplicate" band.


===============================================================================
 5. EXTERNAL / OPEN-SOURCE VALIDATION
===============================================================================
GLEIF LEI REGISTRY  (https://api.gleif.org/api/v1)
  - Free, public, NO API key required. GLEIF is the official global registry of
    Legal Entity Identifiers (LEIs) - 20-character codes for real legal
    entities, used across finance and regulation.
  - What the agent reads from it (code: _lei_record_to_dict):
      * legal name
      * other/former/trading names  (catches rebrands)
      * country and city of the legal address
      * entity status (e.g. ACTIVE) and LEI registration status (e.g. ISSUED)
      * the LEI code itself
  - Hierarchy lookups (code: gleif_parent): /direct-parent and /ultimate-parent.
    This is what lets the agent recognise a SUBSIDIARY as a duplicate of its
    parent group - e.g. "IBM CREDIT LLC" -> ultimate parent "INTERNATIONAL
    BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION" -> matches your existing "IBM" vendor.
  - Multi-field lookups: by LEI code, or by ISIN (a security identifier) which
    GLEIF maps back to the owning entity. (Ticker matching is exact-string only
    and honestly flagged in the code - GLEIF has no ticker concept.)

DUCKDUCKGO WEB-SEARCH FALLBACK (code: cached_web_search, via the "ddgs" library)
  - Intended as a last resort: run a web search only when neither the internal
    master nor GLEIF returned anything, to give a human something to look at.
  - IMPORTANT HONEST FINDING (see section 8): in practice this fallback almost
    never fires, because the internal fuzzy match nearly always returns at least
    one weak candidate, and the fallback only triggers on ZERO candidates.

PERFORMANCE ENGINEERING:
  - In-memory caching of every GLEIF response (including "nothing found") for the
    session, so repeated demo searches are instant.
  - All parent/hierarchy lookups for a search run CONCURRENTLY (thread pool +
    one reused HTTP connection). This takes a cold multi-hit screen from ~12s
    down to ~2-3s, and repeats to well under 1s. (Verified - see section 9.)


===============================================================================
 6. THE FALSE-POSITIVE FIXES  (the "judge-proofing")
===============================================================================
The naive danger: a pure name-matcher gives 100% whenever one name's key words
are contained in another's. That wrongly flags "Infosys" as the same company as
"Capital Infosys", or "Cognizant" as "Cognizant Chemical". Two guards fix this:

FIX 1 - THE CONTAINMENT PENALTY (code: containment_penalty)
  Plain English: if one name is just the other PLUS a different leading word,
  they are probably different companies, so the score is damped.
    - "Infosys" vs "Capital Infosys": different leading word ("Capital") ->
      penalised, so it does NOT read as a duplicate.
    - "Cognizant" vs "Cognizant Technology Solutions": SAME leading word
      ("Cognizant"), just extra descriptors -> NOT penalised (genuine variant).
  So the fix is surgical: it kills the false positive without breaking the true
  brand/legal-name variant.

FIX 2 - THE CORROBORATION GATE (code: the "corroborated" checks)
  Plain English: a name match ALONE can never be a confident duplicate. It must
  be backed by a non-name signal:
    - Internal check: needs a matching country, a matching city, or an exact LEI.
    - External (GLEIF) check: a matching country is deliberately NOT enough (two
      different real companies can share a name in the same country). Promotion
      to "Likely duplicate" needs an IDENTITY-level tie: an exact LEI/ISIN match
      or a corporate-parent link to an existing vendor.
  Anything not corroborated is capped at 84 = "Human review", never "Likely
  duplicate". This is what keeps the false-merge rate low.

WHY "NAMES ALONE ARE NOT ENOUGH": names are cheap to collide on. Requiring a
second, identity-level signal before recommending a merge is what makes the tool
safe to trust with real master data.


===============================================================================
 7. HONEST NOTES / LIMITATIONS  (please read before demo day)
===============================================================================
These are real and were confirmed by reading the code and testing the live app.

1. WEB-SEARCH FALLBACK RARELY (effectively never) FIRES.
   The fallback only runs when the combined candidate list is EMPTY. But the
   internal matcher surfaces any record scoring name-similarity >= 0.55, and
   rapidfuzz's token_set_ratio produces >= 0.55 even for gibberish against short
   names. Verified live: "Zxqwvfoo Traders"/India and "Qzxwptlm Zzyyk"/India
   both returned one weak ~63% "Likely new" candidate and NO web fallback.
   -> The deck's expectation of "nonsense name -> Likely new + web fallback
      present" is only half-true: the verdict is correctly "Likely new", but the
      web fallback does not appear. The feature is implemented and cached
      correctly; the trigger condition is just too strict to reach in practice.
      Easy fix if desired: fire the fallback when the best score is below the
      MEDIUM band, not only when there are zero candidates.

2. WEAK "GHOST" CANDIDATES ON NONSENSE INPUT.
   Because of the same 0.55 threshold, a meaningless name can show one low-score
   (~63%) "Likely new" candidate. It is correctly labelled Likely new / LOW, so
   it does not mislead the recommendation - but it is visual noise. Raising the
   internal reporting floor would tidy this up.

3. RANDOMFOREST IS A SECONDARY CROSS-CHECK ON SYNTHETIC DATA (25% weight).
   The RandomForest (code: train()) is trained on 1,800 RANDOMLY GENERATED
   feature rows, not on real labelled duplicates. It contributes only 25% of the
   internal score; the transparent, explainable weighted heuristic is the
   primary scorer. Present it honestly as a demonstration of the ML path, not as
   the thing making the decisions. With real labelled duplicate/non-duplicate
   history it could be retrained to carry more weight.

4. NO WEBSITE / TAX-ID IN THE PROVIDED DATA.
   The strongest identity signals (domain, tax ID) are absent from Novo's source
   file, so internal corroboration currently leans on country/city/LEI. Adding a
   website or tax-ID column would make the internal check much stronger.

5. GLEIF-ONLY EXTERNAL COVERAGE.
   Only entities with an LEI appear in GLEIF. Many small/local suppliers have no
   LEI, so GLEIF returns nothing for them (correctly, not an error). Broader
   coverage would need additional registries (e.g. national company registers).

6. TICKER MATCHING IS EXACT-STRING ONLY (honestly documented in the code's own
   header). It can confirm two sides both said "TCS" but cannot discover that
   TCS = Tata Consultancy Services. The honest next step is OpenFIGI.

7. STATE IS LIGHTWEIGHT / DEMO-GRADE. GLEIF and web caches are in-memory (lost
   on restart). Human decisions are appended to a local CSV audit log
   (data/decisions_log.csv), not a database.

8. PACKAGING BUG FOUND & FIXED DURING VERIFICATION. run.sh runs
   "pip install -r requirements.txt" but requirements.txt was MISSING from the
   project, so "./run.sh" would fail on a clean machine. A requirements.txt was
   created during this review (fastapi, uvicorn, pydantic, pandas, numpy,
   requests, rapidfuzz, scikit-learn, ddgs) so the documented run path works.


===============================================================================
 8. TECH STACK & ARCHITECTURE
===============================================================================
Language / runtime : Python 3 (single file: main.py, ~1,200 lines)
Web framework      : FastAPI + Uvicorn (ASGI server)
Data / numeric     : pandas, numpy
Fuzzy name match   : rapidfuzz (token_set_ratio, Jaro-Winkler)
ML / TF-IDF        : scikit-learn (RandomForestClassifier, TfidfVectorizer,
                     cosine_similarity)
External registry  : GLEIF LEI API (via requests, connection-pooled, threaded)
Web fallback       : ddgs (DuckDuckGo search)
Front end          : a single self-contained HTML/CSS/JS dashboard served by the
                     app (Novo Nordisk colours), no build step.

ENDPOINTS:
  GET  /            -> the dashboard (HTML).
  POST /screen      -> the core engine. Body: {vendor_name, country, website?,
                       lei?, isin?, ticker?}. Returns candidates with scores,
                       decision labels, evidence, and any web fallback.
  POST /decision    -> records a human's Reuse/Review/Create choice to the CSV
                       audit log. Body includes the query, the chosen candidate,
                       match_percent and decision.
  GET  /decisions   -> lists the recorded decisions (audit trail).
  GET  /health      -> {"status":"ok"} liveness check.

FLOW: /screen -> internal_matches() + external_matches() (GLEIF) -> pool & sort
-> optional web fallback -> JSON -> dashboard renders verdict + evidence bars +
decision buttons -> user clicks -> POST /decision -> CSV audit log.

===============================================================================
 11. ANSWERS TO LIKELY JUDGE QUESTIONS
===============================================================================
Q. What is the business impact / ROI?
   Duplicate vendors split spend across records, causing over-payment risk,
   blind spots in spend analytics, weaker supplier negotiation, and dirty master
   data. Catching duplicates at creation time is far cheaper than downstream
   clean-up. Even a small reduction in duplicate creation compounds across
   thousands of vendors and every future report built on that master.

Q. Does it scale? Can it dedupe other master data?
   Yes. The engine is generic name+attribute matching - the same pipeline can
   dedupe an EMPLOYEE master, a CUSTOMER master, or a materials master by
   pointing it at a different file and choosing the corroborating signals.
   Performance is handled with response caching and concurrent registry lookups;
   the internal check is offline and instant. For very large masters the linear
   scan would be replaced by a blocking/indexing step (a standard next step).

Q. Will it fit Novo's approved tech and adopt easily?
   It is pure Python plus a free, no-key public API (GLEIF) - it fits Novo's
   approved Python / Power BI analytics stack, needs no paid data vendor, and
   runs as a single service with a simple web UI. Decisions are exported to CSV,
   which drops straight into Power BI for the evaluation report.

Q. What about risk of wrongly merging two different companies?
   Low, by design. Name-alone can never reach "Likely duplicate": the
   corroboration gate requires an identity-level second signal (matching
   country/city/LEI internally, or an exact LEI/ISIN or corporate-parent link
   externally), and the containment penalty removes the classic "Capital
   Infosys"/"Cognizant Chemical" false positives - both verified live. When in
   doubt the tool says "Human review", and a human always makes the final call.

Q. Where is the "AI"?
   Three layers: (1) fuzzy/NLP name matching blending three algorithms plus
   acronym expansion; (2) a machine-learning RandomForest cross-check; (3)
   external open-data reasoning over the GLEIF corporate hierarchy to link
   subsidiaries to parents. The design is deliberately EXPLAINABLE - every score
   comes with per-signal evidence, not a black box.

Q. How would you measure success?
   Precision on flagged duplicates (of the pairs it calls "Likely duplicate",
   how many are truly the same company), the false-positive / false-merge rate
   (kept low by the corroboration gate), recall against a labelled set of known
   duplicate pairs, and reviewer time saved per vendor onboarded. The
   /decisions audit log provides the raw data for all of these.
